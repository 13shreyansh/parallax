# Two-minute demo script

Target recording length: 1:55, allowing five seconds of slack. Use the live site. Do not claim a confirmed contradiction or a benchmark win. Check live database status before recording.

**0:00–0:15** Open the worklist on Winter War.

"I'm a Wikipedia editor comparing Finland's history across three languages. Reading three long articles is slow. NPOV Radar gives me a smaller place to start, with the evidence still attached."

**0:15–0:35** Point to the Finnish, English and Swedish passage columns and exact revision links. Open an alternative retrieved passage.

"These are real passages from real revisions. MariaDB's native vector search aligns the passages within the same topic. Different emphasis is valid. The app does not treat language distance as proof of bias."

**0:35–0:55** Select Finnish War, Introduction, and the cited-source panel.

"For this start date, an accessible Yle report supports 21 February 1808. The quote is exact. That verifies this date only. Where we don't have the source evidence, the app says cannot verify."

**0:55–1:15** Open Revision history, choose 2015-01-01, and travel. Show the returned revision ID.

"MariaDB system-versioned tables preserve Wikipedia's actual revision times. This is what the stored article said at that date. Missing archive-to-live periods remain gaps. We don't invent when a disagreement began."

**1:15–1:40** Open Method & evaluation. In the planted test, change a day, then swap a country name.

"Here is a deliberately altered copy of a real retrieved passage. The date change is caught. The name swap is a known miss. These are synthetic tests, never real Wikipedia edits. On fresh held-out articles the rules caught 18 of 20 planted errors, with no flags on 22 controls. This does not measure real-world contradiction accuracy."

**1:40–1:55** Point to the evaluation result and return to the evidence desk.

"We also report the result we didn't win: our small retrieval proxy favored the lexical baseline. The next step is expert labeling, not a bigger accuracy claim. Radar helps the editor inspect, decide and write the review note. It never edits Wikipedia."
